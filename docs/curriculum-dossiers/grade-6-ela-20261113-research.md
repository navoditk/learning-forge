  SL, L), adopted by the SBE August 2, 2010 and modified March 13, 2013.
  No later revision was located through the checks performed. The

  (Grades Six Through Eight), Grade Six section pp. 548–581. This is guidance,
  not additional standards.
- **Current state context:** the 2026 ELA/ELD instructional-materials
  follow-up adoption guidance (December 2025). It does not change the
  standards. It adds media-literacy emphasis that maps onto existing Grade 6
  standards (for example, credibility of sources in W.6.8).

  form (California since 2020–21). The assessment informs emphasis and item
  style only. It does not define the program's mastery evidence, and no
  Smarter Balanced item, stimulus or rubric text may be reproduced.
- **No foundational-skills standards at Grade 6:** the RF strand ends at
  grade 5. The framework treats fluency as continuing support. Any
  fluency/decoding support would be a product choice, not a Grade 6
  standards claim.

Own-summary topics by strand (orientation only):

- **RL / RI 1–3 (key ideas and details):** citing evidence for explicit
  and inferred meaning; theme or central idea with an objective summary;
  plot and character change (RL) or how a key person, event or idea is
  introduced and developed (RI).
- **RL / RI 4–6 (craft and structure):** word and phrase meaning in context
  (figurative, connotative, technical); how a part fits the whole structure;
  RI.6.5a adds text features in popular media; narrator/speaker point of
  view (RL) or author's purpose and point of view (RI).
- **RL / RI 7–9 (integration):** comparing reading with an audio, video or
  live version (RL.6.7); integrating information across formats (RI.6.7);
  tracing an argument and separating supported from unsupported claims
  (RI.6.8); comparing genres on a shared theme (RL.6.9) or two authors'
  accounts of the same events (RI.6.9).
- **W 1–3 (text types):** argument with claim, reasons and credible evidence
  in a formal style; informative/explanatory writing with a thesis statement,
  organizing strategies, transitions and domain vocabulary; narrative with
  context, narrator, dialogue, pacing, description and transitions.
- **W 4–6 (production):** writing suited to task, purpose and audience;
  planning, revising, editing and rewriting with guidance; technology use,
  including typing at least three pages in a single sitting.
- **W 7–9 (research):** short research projects; gathering and assessing
  sources, quoting or paraphrasing without plagiarism and giving basic
  bibliographic information; drawing evidence from texts for analysis.
- **SL 1–6:** collaborative discussion; interpreting media; delineating a
  speaker's argument; presenting with eye contact, volume and pronunciation,
  including a planned informative presentation (SL.6.4a); multimedia; formal
  register.
- **L 1–3:** pronoun case, intensive pronouns, pronoun shifts and vague
  pronouns, recognizing nonstandard variation; punctuation for
  nonrestrictive/parenthetical elements; spelling; sentence variety and
  consistent style and tone. The Language Progressive Skills table marks
  L.6.1c–e, L.6.2a and L.6.3a–b as skills likely to need continued attention
  in later grades.
- **L 4–6:** context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, reference
  materials, verifying inferred meanings; figurative language, word
  relationships and connotation; academic and domain-specific vocabulary.
- **Standard 10:** RL.6.10 (p. 48) and RI.6.10 (p. 52) require proficient
  year-end comprehension of literature and of literary nonfiction within
  the grades 6–8 complexity band, with scaffolding as needed at the high end.
  W.6.10 (p. 60) is a separate writing-frequency expectation over extended
  and shorter time frames. Page 77 names three complexity factors:
  qualitative, quantitative, and reader and task.
- **Standard 10 range of text types (p. 77):** stories (including several named
  subgenres), drama, poetry and literary nonfiction from a broad range of
  cultures and periods. Drama and poetry carry CA markers.

### Content tiers, originality, and licensing constraints

- **Proposed tiers:** `core` and `depth`; no `contest` tier (spelling-bee
  preparation is a separate program).
  - `core`: every text-feasible Grade 6 standard above, practised on
    original or verified public-domain passages. This includes the RL.6.10
    and RI.6.10 requirement to read proficiently across the whole grades
    6–8 complexity band, including its high end with scaffolding.
  - `depth`: optional extensions beyond the Grade 6 requirements only.
    Candidates, all pending decision ELA-D8: additional texts beyond the
    core set, cross-text synthesis beyond what RL.6.9 and RI.6.9 require,
    extra revision cycles, and media-literacy extensions beyond the Grade 6
    standards (decision ELA-D11).
  - Invariant: `depth` never contains a Grade 6 requirement that is left
    out of `core`, and it is never a prerequisite for core mastery. Reading
    high-end band texts with scaffolding is core, not depth.
- **Style inspiration (described, never copied):** the framework's
  instructional approaches (close reading, evidence-based discussion
  prompts, summary scaffolds, planning → drafting → revising) and the
  general _item types_ of the state assessment (passage sets with
  selected and short-text responses; brief-write and revise tasks; a
  source-based performance task scored on organization/purpose,
  evidence/elaboration and conventions). These inform format only.
- **Originality confirmation:** no text, passages, questions, prompts,
  rubrics, student writing samples, snapshots, vignettes, figures or answer
  keys from any of the following will be reproduced or closely
  paraphrased:
  - the CDE framework;
  - CCSS Appendices A–C (not retrieved; Appendix B text exemplars include
    copyrighted works);
  - Smarter Balanced blueprints, specifications, practice/interim items or
    rubrics;
  - IUSD documents;
  - StudySync or any other publisher material;
  - any third-party site.
- **Passages:** the only proposed routes are
  - (a) original passages written for the program; and
  - (b) public-domain works whose US status has been recorded per work by
    a human (decision ELA-D2).

  The standards' illustrative 6–8 text list (p. 78) mixes works that are
  likely public domain because of their age with works that are clearly
  still in copyright. Inclusion on that list grants no reuse right.
  Translations and modern editions can carry separate rights. No source
  in this dossier is "licensed".

- **Standards text itself:** © 2013 CDE (all rights reserved; resale not
  authorized), with the CCSS portion under an NGA/CCSSO public license whose
  current terms could not be verified. Proposal: store only standard codes
  plus own short summaries until decision ELA-D3 resolves.
- **Provenance:** model-assisted drafts are `provenance.origin:
"llm_drafted"` with `review.status: "pending_review"`, per
  `docs/content-review.md` and `docs/content-authoring-pipeline.md`.
  - Never promote such drafts to `original` by model or independent review.
  - Use `original` only for evidence-supported human authorship.
  - Mark public-domain passages with their verified source; whether they
    use a distinct provenance value is an authoring-contract question, not
    decided here.

### Accessibility and age-appropriateness

- WCAG 2.2 AA applies. Passages need adjustable text, screen-reader
  structure and no reliance on colour. Any graphic or media stimulus needs a
  text alternative. Audio, if ever added, needs transcripts and captions.
- Typing load: W.6.6 expects three pages in one sitting. Session design must
  respect the product's workload limits and must not penalize slower
  typists in mastery evidence.
- Passage content must be age-appropriate. Public-domain works can contain
  dated or offensive portrayals. Each selected excerpt needs human review
  for this, with context or exclusion as appropriate.
- Narrative writing (W.6.3, "real or imagined") invites personal
  disclosure. Prompts should steer toward imagined or non-identifying
  content (see ELA-O11 and decision ELA-D16).
