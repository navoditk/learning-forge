### Standards framework and version

- **Governing standards:** California History–Social Science Content
  Standards, Grade Six, adopted by the SBE on October 9, 1998. The 2017
  adoption report states that these standards had not changed when the
  2016 framework was written. No later revision was located through the
  checks performed. The CDE landing page that would show revisions was
  bot-challenged.
- **Current framework:** History–Social Science Framework, adopted July
  2016 and published 2017, Chapter 10. The exact adoption day is not
  printed on the chapter pages read. The adoption report places the
  framework's approval in July 2016.
- **Analysis skills:** Grades 6–8 Historical and Social Sciences Analysis
  Skills (CST 1–3, REP 1–5, HI 1–6). They are required alongside the
  content standards, not separately.
- **Literacy:** CA CCSS RH.6–8.1–10 and WHST.6–8 (modified March 2013).
  These adopted disciplinary literacy standards supplement, not replace,
  the content standards. Reading and writing support aligned to them is
  mandatory and embedded in History–Social Science tasks. They are a
  support layer, not History–Social Science content or mastery claims.

### Sequencing source and authority

- **Authority for order:** the content-standard numbering 6.1 → 6.7 and the
  matching Chapter 10 section order. Both are official, but neither
  mandates instructional pacing. The prerequisite graph may follow 6.1 →
  6.7, with analysis skills spiraled throughout.
- **Local pacing:** none used. No district or school pacing document was
  sought or cited, per the user's instruction.
- **IMPACT order (partial, metadata only):**
  - The 2017 IQC review copy cites Grade 6 "Chp 1: Early Humans and the
    Agricultural Revolution" and an online "Ch. 7: Ancient India", and it
    refers to Chapters 10 and 11 without naming them.
  - The public publisher sample shows Chapter 5, "The Ancient Greeks", at
    student pages 155–203.
  - Together these suggest the IMPACT order may place Hebrews and/or Greece
    before India. It may also include material beyond standard 6.7, since
    the IQC report mentions a Palenque video note. This is **not verified**.
  - The full chapter list, the chapters' standards mapping, and whether the
    2019-copyright edition kept the 2017 sample/review numbering all remain
    open.
  - Do not invent chapter numbers or titles. Until a legally accessible
    table of contents is read, map skills to standard codes only, with an
    optional `impactChapter` alignment value left unset.

### Content tiers, originality, and licensing constraints

- **`core`:** original explanations, maps described in the authors' own
  words, timelines, vocabulary and practice, with embedded RH/WHST 6–8
  reading and writing support. Cover every one of the 48
  substandards, each paired with at least one analysis skill.
- **`depth`:** original tasks for sourcing and corroboration, causation,
  comparison across regions, geography–environment reasoning, change over
  time, and the framework-only optional extras (not 6.4.5 Persian Empire
  coverage, which is core). Depth must never be a
  prerequisite for core mastery.
- **Inspiration only:** the framework's guiding questions and the general
  inquiry style of the CA History–Social Science framework. Content must be
  original, not adapted from framework classroom-example prose.
- **Prohibited:** copying or closely paraphrasing IMPACT text, lesson
  openers, "story" vignettes, primary-source selections as excerpted by the
  publisher, captions, maps, timelines, diagrams, Foldables, questions,
  assessments or answer keys. The same applies to Inquiry Journal and
  Weekly Explorer content. Lesson titles listed above are recorded as
  alignment metadata only.
- **Primary sources in lessons:** use only short attributed excerpts whose
  public-domain or license status an adult reviewer has recorded, or use
  original summaries that are clearly labeled. Translations can carry
  separate rights. Museum and image reuse needs recorded terms for each
  object. None was verified in this research, so no source here is
  "licensed".
- **Provenance:** model-assisted drafts are `provenance.origin:
"llm_drafted"` with `review.status: "pending_review"`, per
  `docs/content-review.md` and `docs/content-authoring-pipeline.md`.
  Original wording is not the same as authorship provenance. Model or
  independent review never rewrites the origin to `original`, and human
  approval stays a separate recorded gate. Use `original` only for
  evidence-supported human authorship. Nothing qualifies as `licensed`.

### Accessibility, age-appropriateness, and religion neutrality

- Follow framework Appendix F for every unit that teaches belief systems
  (6.2.3, 6.3, 6.4.4, 6.5.3–6.5.5, 6.6.3–6.6.4, 6.6.8, 6.7.6–6.7.7):
  - Present beliefs academically, with attribution: say "according to the
    Hebrew Bible", "Buddhist tradition holds", or "the New Testament
    describes".
  - Distinguish historical evidence from tradition or scripture.
  - No ranking of religions, devotional framing, role-play of rituals, or
    "we/they" language.
- Present legendary or mythical figures named in the standards (for
  example Aeneas, Romulus and Remus) as tradition, separate from evidenced
  history.
- Treat contested topics as contested and source each claim:
  - Standard 6.5.2 uses the wording "Aryan invasions". The Chapter 10 text
    extracted for this dossier does not use the term "Aryan". How to frame
    that standard is a human decision (see decision 5). Use current,
    reviewed scholarship and avoid racialized framing.
  - The caste-system topic in 6.5.4. Chapter 10 describes jatis and varnas
    as a social and cultural structure. It notes that many Hindus today do
    not identify as belonging to a caste and that elites used religion to
    justify hierarchy. Content must not equate present-day Hindus with
    ancient hierarchy.
  - Slavery, warfare and conquest, presented age-appropriately and without
    graphic detail.
- Use BCE/CE consistently, as the framework does.
- Provide text alternatives and non-color cues for maps and timelines,
  keyboard-operable map and timeline interactions, and reading-level
  support. The WCAG 2.2 AA target applies.
- Give culturally responsible, non-Eurocentric framing that reflects the
  framework's world-history emphasis. Do not stereotype modern communities
  through ancient topics.
